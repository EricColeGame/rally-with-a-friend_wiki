## Team Roles: Driver vs. Co-Driver Communication Strategies

Success in **RALLY WITH A FRIEND friend mode** hinges entirely on verbal brevity and shared spatial awareness. A driver traveling at 110 miles per hour across unpaved surfaces cannot digest long, flowery descriptions. Calls must follow standard rally grammar: Direction, Severity, Distance, and Immediate Hazard.

When playing as a navigator, speaking at a measured tempo ahead of the car's current physical position is paramount. Delivering a callout as the driver enters the turn is already too late; pace notes should generally arrive between one and two corners ahead of the vehicle's position.

| Corner Rating Callout | Steering Lock Angle | Approximate Speed Range | Crucial Driver Reaction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Turn 1 (Acute/Hairpin)** | Full Lock (80°–90°) | 15–25 mph (24–40 km/h) | Heavy braking, handbrake slide, first gear |
| **Turn 2 (Tight 90°)** | Severe Lock (65°–80°) | 25–40 mph (40–64 km/h) | Firm brake trail, downshift to second gear |
| **Turn 3 (Medium-Slow)** | Moderate (45°–65°) | 40–55 mph (64–88 km/h) | Controlled weight transfer, third gear throttle hold |
| **Turn 4 (Medium-Fast)** | Gentle (30°–45°) | 55–70 mph (88–112 km/h) | Light tap on brake, maintain line momentum |
| **Turn 5 (Fast Flowing)** | Slight (15°–30°) | 70–85 mph (112–136 km/h) | Throttle feathering without heavy braking |
| **Turn 6 (Flat Out)** | Minimal (0°–15°) | Top Gear / Maximum Speed | Full throttle commitment through apex |

### Supplemental Callout Modifiers

Alongside basic turn numbers, navigators must seamlessly integrate key descriptors:
- **"Don't Cut":** Indicates exposed boulders, anti-cut posts, or drainage gullies lurking on the inside shoulder.
- **"Crest / Jump":** Warns the driver that road elevation will unload the suspension, compromising braking authority.
- **"Tightens":** Flags that the radius decreases mid-corner, requiring a late brake or tightened steering arc.
- **"Opens":** Informs the pilot that they can feed in throttle early toward track exit.

Establishing these verbal shorthands in **RALLY WITH A FRIEND friend mode** prevents sudden catastrophic terminal damage and shaves decisive seconds off your combined overall split times.

### Handling Mid-Stage Mechanical Attrition

Community reports often emphasize that conservative pace outlasts reckless speed. When racing cooperatively across multi-stage loops, minor mechanical damage compounds quickly:
- **Punctures:** If a tire pops early in a long stage, evaluate whether pulling over to spend 30 seconds mounting a spare yields better overall results than limping on three wheels and wrecking your suspension strut.
- **Radiator Leaks:** A light front-end brush with a tree can puncture the radiator. Driving flat out will cause engine overheating and seize the motor; back off throttle application to keep coolant temperatures manageable.
- **Gearbox Synchro Wear:** Aggressive manual downshifts without rev-matching damage transmission gears. Instruct your driver to smooth out their pedal cadence if gears begin slipping.

Coordinating these tactical repairs during service stops ensures both partners finish the final stage without sustaining retirement-inducing penalties.
